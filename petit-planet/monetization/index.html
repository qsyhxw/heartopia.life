Separate character access from cosmetic purchases

In a November 9 interview with Pocket Tactics, lead game designer Murphy described the core experience, NPC interactions and multiplayer as accessible to all players. She said monetization would focus on cosmetics, with all Neighbors and their stories available free. She also explained that the monetization used in earlier tests was still being revised.

Read the developer interview for these statements. This is the developer's pre-release description. It does not supply a complete final price list, drop-rate table or spending ceiling.

Why older guides disagree

Fanstanza's Stardrift hands-on account describes direct cosmetic purchases and a pass. Its November 3 revision adds the later Final Beta cosmetic draw system. For a decision now, combine the current store disclosure with the developers' more recent statements. Recheck rates and item terms in the release build before using a beta-era currency conversion or purchase plan.

What to check before spending

At release, inspect the actual shop terms for the item you want. Establish whether it is selected directly or obtained randomly. For a random reward, check the displayed probability and any stated guarantee. For a pass, check what buying it immediately grants and what still requires progression or another currency.

Final prices and guarantees have not been verified. Check the release shop's own terms before deciding whether to buy.

What the free beta proves

The official Final Beta guide says that test had no in-game purchases. This establishes the test's purchase policy. The developer interview separately describes plans for paid cosmetics at launch.
